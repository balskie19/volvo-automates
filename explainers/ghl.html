Follow-up that runs on its own.

The CRM is where the follow-up lives. These are the builds that decide who gets messaged, who gets left alone, and who is worth interrupting you for. Each one plays from its trigger to its last step, the same shape as the canvas it was built on. Press play, or just scroll - they start when they reach you.

When someone fills in a form

The moment somebody asks for something, the record is already right.

3 builds

A purchase turns a lead into a customer

An order comes in. The old label comes off, the new one goes on, the deal moves down the pipeline, and the confirmation goes out.

Ready

Worth noticing. The lead label is removed BEFORE the customer label is added. Left on, a paying customer keeps getting the emails meant for people who have not bought.

A form becomes a quote

Someone asks for a price. The numbers are worked out elsewhere and handed back, the contact is created, and the quote is emailed.

Ready

Worth noticing. The quote is built by a separate system and handed back. One job per tool, so neither becomes the thing nobody can safely change.

Six emails, spaced out, then it stops

A new contact gets six emails days apart: hello, the story, the useful one, the offer, the proof, the objections.

Ready

Worth noticing. It ends. A sequence with no end is not nurture, it is spam with a schedule.

When a call is booked

Booked is a different state from interested, and it is treated as one.

1 build

Booked, then reminded four times

A confirmed booking is labelled, moved down the pipeline, and reminded at three days, two days, the day before and on the day.

Ready

Worth noticing. The step that matters is the fourth one: it takes them OUT of the chase. Without it the person who just booked keeps receiving messages asking them to book.

A comment becomes a conversation

Someone comments on a post. It waits, replies publicly, waits again, then opens a private message with a way to go further.

Ready

Worth noticing. It pauses before replying. An instant answer to a comment reads as a robot, and the pause costs nothing.

The same question, asked two ways

A comment and a direct message are the same intent arriving differently, so they get different handling and end in the same place.

Ready

Worth noticing. One of the endings is simply 'not for me', and it is taken at face value rather than answered with another offer.

The conversation engines

Two systems that hold a real back-and-forth by text. Five stages each, and an exit each.

3 builds

Speed to Lead: answer before they cool

A brand new lead gets a message within moments, then up to five stages of conversation, and a human takes over the second it is worth a human.

Ready

Worth noticing. It never tries to close. Its whole job is to reach a person who is still interested and hand them over warm.

Waking up leads everyone gave up on

Old leads nobody has time to call twice get re-opened by text, qualified over five stages, and only then handed a calendar link.

Ready

Worth noticing. The calendar link is gated. Sending it to everyone turns a booking page into a spam complaint.

One word, and everything stops

The customer replies quit. Messages stop, and they are removed from all five stages at once.

Ready

Worth noticing. Three separate triggers watch for Quit, QUIT and quit, because a person who wants out will not check their capital letters first. This ships with BOTH engines.

Only tell me when they actually qualify

A record changes. If the person genuinely qualifies they are labelled, their intent is noted, the deal moves, and you are told. Otherwise nothing happens.

Ready

Worth noticing. Silence is the correct outcome most of the time. A notification for every change is a notification nobody reads.

Ready
