The wiring between everything else.

Make is where the tools that were never designed to work together are made to. A form here, a document there, a record somewhere else - joined up. Each one plays from its trigger to its last step, the same shape as the canvas it was built on. Press play, or just scroll - they start when they reach you.

When a form is answered

The answer does more than land in an inbox.

4 builds

Their answers become a document, emailed back

Someone fills in a form. What they wrote is turned into a document made for them, and it is in their inbox while they are still on the page.

Ready

Worth noticing. It is built from their answers, not a template with their name dropped in.

One form, two very different follow-ups

The same form is answered by people who paid and people who only replied, and each is treated differently from that moment on.

Ready

Worth noticing. Someone who only answered is not chased like someone who bought.

A form becomes a signed document, filed

An answer draws up the document, fetches the finished copy, files it where the team already looks, and logs it.

Ready

Worth noticing. Filed AND logged. A document nobody can find later was not really filed.

Two documents, two recipients

One answer fills a template twice: the customer's copy and the internal copy, each sent to the right place.

Ready

Worth noticing. They are not the same document, and treating them as one is how the wrong version reaches a customer.

When another system calls in

Something finishes elsewhere and this picks it up.

3 builds

Write the text and the email, then send

One event produces both a short message for a phone and a longer one for an inbox, then sends it.

Ready

Worth noticing. Same facts, different shape. A text that reads like an email gets deleted.

A booking changes, and the record keeps up

Cancelled, started or moved - three different things, each written onto the person's record properly.

Ready

Worth noticing. Three outcomes, three paths. Collapsing them loses the reason the record changed.

Tell the voice agent what time it is

A voice agent has no clock of its own. This answers with the current time in the caller's timezone.

Ready

Worth noticing. Without it an agent will cheerfully offer an appointment slot that has already passed.

On a clock

Running whether anyone is at a desk or not.

12 builds

The email that goes out without anyone sending it

Reads the next row, fills in the blanks, pauses, and sends.

Ready

Worth noticing. The pause is deliberate. Sent all at once it looks like a blast, and gets treated like one.

Look at the picture, write the caption, post it

Finds the next image, actually looks at it, writes a caption from what is in it, marks it used, and posts.

Ready

Worth noticing. It marks the image used BEFORE posting. Crash after posting and you have a duplicate; crash after marking and you have a gap, which is the cheaper mistake.

One post a day, without a person

The same idea on a daily clock for a different platform.

Ready

Worth noticing. Once a day at a fixed time, so the account looks tended rather than automated.

Read the news, draft the post

New articles get a draft written about them, and only the good ones go out. The rest are kept to finish later.

Ready

Worth noticing. Not everything drafted is worth posting, and the ones that are not still get kept.

New name or old name, and the team is told

Checks each row against the records, adds a note to people already known, creates the ones who are not, and announces only the new ones.

Ready

Worth noticing. An existing customer arriving again is not a new lead, and announcing them as one costs the team's trust in the alerts.

Clean up a list of phone numbers

Fetches records, works through them one at a time, puts every number into the same shape, and writes them down.

Ready

Worth noticing. One shape means a list you can dial from rather than one somebody has to fix first.

Every product gets its folder

Runs each afternoon, checks whether each product already has a folder, and creates only the missing ones.

Ready

Worth noticing. Doing nothing is the usual outcome, and that is the point of checking first.

Read the ad copy and score it

Finds copy waiting for review, reads it, turns the verdict into columns, and writes it back to the same row.

Ready

Worth noticing. Prose into columns, so a hundred pieces can be sorted instead of read.

Ad copy in three languages

One piece of copy translated into English, German and French, each written back into its own column.

Ready

Worth noticing. Three markets from one write-up, with the original kept beside them.

The Thursday reminder, worded differently

Loads the reminders, shuffles the wording, and posts it to the team every Thursday.

Ready

Worth noticing. The shuffle is the point. The same words every week stop being read by about the fourth one.

Collect offices, skip the ones on file

Runs a collector, waits for it to finish, takes the results, and adds only the ones not already recorded.

Ready

Worth noticing. It waits rather than guessing how long collecting takes, and it never adds the same office twice.

Line up the call, then place it

Finds who to call, checks nobody is rung twice, reads the real diary for free slots, hands the details over, and places the call.

Ready

Worth noticing. It reads the actual diary first, so the agent can offer a time that genuinely exists.
