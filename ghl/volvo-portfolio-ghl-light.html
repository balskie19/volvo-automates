How it goes

One process, eating one week.

Week 0

We find the bottleneck

Twenty minutes on a call. You describe what eats the most time, I tell you whether it is worth automating, and I say so when it is not.

Week 1

I wire one process end to end

Not a platform, not a transformation. One process, working, in your own tools, where you can watch it run.

Ongoing

You keep it, and it keeps running

Handover with the runbook. If it breaks it tells somebody, because a silent failure is worse than no automation at all.

Who this is for

Two people hire me, for opposite reasons.

01 · The agency owner

You sold it. Now it has to exist.

The retainer is signed and the build is queued behind three other things. I build it under your name, on your stack, and I do not appear on the client call unless you want me there.

02 · The service business

You do not need more software.

Leads arrive, somebody retypes them into three places, and the one that came in at 9pm is gone by morning. What is missing is not a tool. It is the thing between the tools, and nobody owns it.

Not for: brochure sites, logos, or running your ads. I do the plumbing, not the paint. If what you need is a designer, I will tell you that on the first call rather than in the third week.

Why it stays broken

Every tool you own has an owner. The space between them does not.

Your CRM has a rep. Your forms have a vendor. Your ad platform has a dashboard and somebody whose job is that dashboard. The handoffs between them belong to nobody, so they get done by a person instead, quietly, forever. That gap is the entire job.

Nobody is measured on it

No vendor loses a renewal because two of your systems disagree.

It never fails loudly

Nothing alerts. A lead just does not get called, and the month still closes.

Your best person absorbs it

The most reliable person you employ quietly becomes the integration.

The awkward questions

Ask the thing you were not going to ask.

We tried automation before and it broke.

Usually it did not break, it stopped being watched. Anything I build reports to a channel you already read, and says so when it fails. A silent automation is worse than none, which is why failure is a feature I build on purpose.

Our setup is complicated.

Good. Simple setups rarely need me. I start by wiring one process end to end rather than mapping everything, so you see it working in week one instead of reading a discovery document in month three.

We already know the problem, we need someone to fix it.

Then we skip the audit. Send me the process, I will tell you on the call whether it is worth automating, and I will tell you when it is not.

Why you and not an agency?

An agency sells you a retainer and a project manager. You get the person who builds it. And every build I have shipped is on this site with the part that went wrong, which is not a thing a brochure can fake.

We do not have budget for this.

Then the honest answer may be no. If the process does not cost you real hours or real leads, automating it is a hobby. Bring the one that hurts and we will size it in twenty minutes.

Can you work with the tools we already have?

That is the whole job. GoHighLevel, HubSpot, n8n, Make, Zapier, Apify, Slack, OpenPhone, Intercom, Monday. If it has an API or a webhook, it can be wired in. If it does not, I build the missing piece.
